# v0.4.0: Payment, Shipping, Compensation, Cancellation, and Interventions (M3)

Milestone M3 of the PoC. Synthetic data only; not production software.
Everything of v0.3.0 (durable acceptance, execution fence, operator review,
inventory with stock actors and guarded SQL) is still there; the simulated
payment and shipment steps of v0.3.0 are replaced by real services against
external simulators. See [v0.3.0.md](v0.3.0.md).

## What It Does

- **Payment service and an external payment provider.** The payment service
  owns `PaymentDb`; the provider is a separate simulator with its own
  database and no Dapr sidecar. Every request (authorize, capture, void,
  refund) is sent with an operation key and a payload hash; the payment
  service records an intent and an attempt row before each send. A request
  without an answer is reconciled by status query; a refused connection
  counts as nothing sent. Outcomes that are not definite end as
  `Unavailable` (provably nothing sent) or `Ambiguous` (an intervention case).
- **Compensation by key.** A void or refund names the operation it
  compensates by that operation's key. Against an operation that never
  committed it is a committed no-op that prohibits the key, inside the
  provider's database, so a late original can never commit afterwards; a
  capture is refused after a void.
- **Fulfillment with payment and shipment.** Reserve the lines, authorize,
  pack (one simulated step per line), start the dispatch (the point of no
  return), extend the holds once, capture, consume the holds, dispatch to
  the carrier, wait for delivery, and complete with an `OrderCompleted`
  outbox row.
- **Holds and the capture.** Before the capture the order's holds are
  extended once (30 min; drill 30 s), never beyond the authorization's
  expiry and never shortened. The capture requires enough remaining hold
  validity; consumption checks expiry again; a hold lost after the capture
  is refunded.
- **Shipping and an external carrier.** The shipping service owns
  `ShippingDb` with the carrier status history and a current-state rule
  (versions only move forward; the first terminal status wins). The carrier
  simulator keeps durable jobs and sends HMAC-signed callbacks. The order
  API's ingress writes an inbox row and a forward command in one
  transaction; a leased forwarder applies them through Shipping and stores
  the outcome (`Applied`, `Stale`, `Duplicate`, `TerminalApplied`,
  `TerminalConflict`, `CorrelationMismatch`, ...). Only a terminal status
  wakes the waiting workflow, which reads Shipping instead of trusting the
  signal; at the delivery deadline it asks the carrier once.
- **Unfulfillable orders.** Holds are released first, then the payment is
  voided (authorization key) or refunded (capture key) in rounds with the
  same key; when the rounds run out, a `CompensationExhausted` case waits
  for an operator. After the dispatch there is no compensation: an
  undeterminable dispatch, a delivery exception, or an overdue delivery
  opens a case.
- **Cancellation.** `POST /orders/{id}/cancel` before the dispatch
  starts is recorded and honoured by the workflow, including while it waits
  for a review; afterwards it answers `409 dispatch-started` (a terminal
  order that never started dispatch: `409 order-terminal`).
- **Review lifecycle.** Manual escalation of a round before its deadline;
  new compatible `409` codes `review-cancelled`, `no-further-round`.
- **Intervention cases.** `AmbiguousPayment`, `CompensationExhausted`,
  `AmbiguousDispatch`, `DeliveryException`, `DeliveryOverdue`,
  `CarrierTerminalConflict`, one open case per order and kind. A resolution
  takes exactly one path: a signal to the waiting workflow, a
  `RepairOrderWorkflow` with its own keys (started by its own leased
  dispatcher) when no workflow waits, or record only for a terminal order.
  After a payment case the workflow reconciles with the provider; the
  provider's record wins over the operator's evidence.
- **Client simulator.** `cancel`, `reviews`, `escalate`, `resolve`, and a
  seeded scripted operator (`operator run`) whose plan and digest depend
  only on seed, weights, operators, and order ids.

## Verified Guarantees

Proven by scenario scripts and failure drills that print `PASS`/`FAIL` from
real runs on a stack with two or three replicas per role, injecting real
failures (process kills, failpoints, provider and carrier faults) and
reading every database.

| Guarantee | Evidence |
|---|---|
| A lost provider answer (authorize or capture) is adopted by status query: one provider operation, no second charge | R09 (S38) |
| A provider outage before sending compensates the order; a late original against the voided key is refused | R33 A (S50) |
| An answer lost with the provider down becomes an `AmbiguousPayment` case and is reconciled with the provider; a provider crash after committing a capture is adopted once it is back | R33 B, C |
| An undeterminable payment is resolved correctly while the workflow waits (signal) and after it was terminated (repair with its own void key) | R26 (S37) |
| A declined authorization rejects the order and releases its holds | S13 |
| An interrupted compensation finishes; one that cannot finish surfaces as a case and completes after resolution | R10 (S14) |
| A hold lost before the capture leads to a void, one lost after it to a refund; nothing is captured against an expired authorization | S40, S41 |
| A carrier crash after accepting a shipment creates one shipment, and its callbacks still complete the order | R11 (S25, S26) |
| Duplicate, early, stale, conflicting, wrong-order, and badly signed callbacks are recorded and harmless; one completion | R13 (S10, S27) |
| A conflicting terminal status after completion, also with a forwarder crash, is recorded once and becomes one case; the order stays `Completed` | R31 (S47) |
| Cancellation racing the dispatch start: either cancelled with nothing dispatched or dispatched with the cancellation refused | R27 (20 races), S39 |
| Two operators deciding at once on different replicas: one winner; a repeated decision is a replay | R22, R23 (decision part), S33, S34 |

## Known Limitations

- **A failed delivery has no business end yet.** When a delivery fails after
  the dispatch (a carrier `Exception`, or an overdue delivery confirmed as
  failed), the intervention case makes it visible, and only
  `ConfirmExternalSuccess` completes the order. Any other resolution is
  recorded, and the order returns to `AwaitingDelivery`: the payment stays
  captured, the stock stays consumed, and nothing refunds, reships, restocks,
  or books a loss. Such orders must be found by their resolved cases and
  handled outside the system until a business policy is defined.
- **Hold lifetime versus pauses.** One bounded extension before the capture
  covers ordinary payment and dispatch times. A pause longer than the
  remaining lifetime (for example a long-open intervention case before the
  capture) still lets the holds expire; the order is then compensated
  (voided, or refunded after a capture), never oversold or captured
  without valid holds.
- **Reason code of an expired authorization.** The check before the capture
  reads the holds' remaining validity, which the extension caps at the
  authorization's expiry; the provider enforces the authorization's validity
  on its own, so an expired authorization never yields a committed capture.
  The order's reason code is then `ReservationExpired` or
  `PaymentCaptureFailed`; no code names the expired authorization.
- **No automatic recovery of stranded workflows.** A workflow that failed or
  vanished is not restarted (M4 reconciler); its signals end `Stuck`, and
  an intervention case can be resolved through the repair path.
- **Outbox events are written but not published** (`OrderCompleted`, hold
  events): event distribution, history, projections, and reconciliation are
  M4. Nothing in this release depends on them.
- A carrier callback that exhausts its retries is dead-lettered at the
  carrier simulator and not redelivered; the workflow still asks the carrier
  once at the delivery deadline.
- Dapr service invocation has deliberately no retry policy for application
  errors; the callers (forwarder, payment and shipping clients) retry
  themselves and the receivers are idempotent.
- Packing is still a simulated step per line; no partial shipment, partial
  capture, or partial refund; one line per SKU per order.
- Unchanged from v0.3.0: the execution view does not show inventory rows;
  no restock path; stock actor latency not measured; a few seconds of
  failing invocations after a workflow-host replica restarts; the
  intermittent integration-test failure at fixture start; local Docker
  Compose only, static API keys, no Dapr mTLS.

## Deferred

- **M4:** outbox relays and publication, broker subscriptions and dead
  letters, event history and current-state projections with gap repair, the
  reconciler (stranded workflows and signals, nudges), carrier forwarder
  hardening.
- **M5:** alerts and dashboards, performance and scaling runs, workflow
  upgrades, Kubernetes.
- **Open business decision:** what a failed delivery means for the order
  (refund, reshipment, or loss).
